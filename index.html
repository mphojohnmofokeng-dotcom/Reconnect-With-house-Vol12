The session

House music didn’t appear from nowhere.

The records were already dancing before house had a name.

Reconnect With House Vol.12 follows a musical conversation rather than a strict era. South African house, New York soulful house, gospel, neo-soul, disco, boogie, jazz and broken-beat ideas meet in the same room.

The point is not to make the records sound the same. It is to hear why they belong together.

The 13-record journey

Open the crate.

Tap a record. Find out where it came from, what is documented about it, and why it is here.

01

Sir LSG — Departure

South African house · Thandi Ntuli · GOGO Music · 2017

+

The record

Departure opens Sir LSG’s Moving Circles, released in 2017 on GOGO Music, and features Thandi Ntuli. The album was deliberately conceived as a journey through love, life, inspiration, sadness and loneliness — beginning with “Departure” and eventually reaching “Arrival.”

Why it is here

This is the doorway. I wanted Vol.12 to begin in South Africa, with a record that already understands that house can be spacious, emotional and musical without losing the groove.

Sources: DECKS · Amazon Music

02

McGee Keys — I Appreciate You (feat. Khathu)

Contemporary soul · Art of Life EP · 2025

+

The record

I Appreciate You appears on McGee Keys’ five-track Art of Life EP from 2025, featuring Khathu. The documented release places the track firmly in the contemporary part of this crate.

Why it is here

I wanted the session to acknowledge that soulful house is still being written now. This keeps the mix from becoming a museum piece — the old records speak, but the new records answer back.

Sources: Apple Music · Amazon Music

03

Blaze — Here With Me

Soulful house · LifeLine Records · 2005

+

The record

Released by Blaze on LifeLine Records in 2005, Here With Me was described at the time as unusually relaxed and elegant for the duo, with sweet Latin percussion and a strong soulful-dance character rather than a hard club attack.

Why it is here

This is the kind of Blaze I keep coming back to: warm, patient and musical. It gives the mix room to breathe and reminds us that deep house does not need to shout to move a room.

Source: Spirit of House, 2005 review

04

Andy Compton — I Belong (feat. Celestine)

Organic soulful house · Celestine · 2015

+

The record

I Belong was released as a single in 2015 and later appeared in Celestine and Andy Compton’s Peng Moments collection. The documented credits list Celestine, Andy Compton, Shamrock and Bongani Mazibuko as writers and producers.

Why it is here

The title says a lot about the feeling I wanted. This is music that feels connected to people, place and musicianship rather than simply to a dance-floor function. It is one of the records that makes the mix feel human.

Sources: Apple Music · Celestine / Bandcamp

05

Vick Lavender — All of Me & All of You

V.L.E. Main Vocal Mix · Spike Rebel · Marcus Austin · 2021

+

The record

Vick Lavender’s V.L.E. Main Vocal Mix features Spike Rebel and Marcus Austin. It was released through Vibe Boutique Records in March 2021 and runs for nearly twelve minutes — the kind of long-form soulful-house record built to let a groove develop.

Why it is here

This is where I wanted the set to settle deeper into the pocket. The vocal, the length and the patience make it feel like a record you discover rather than consume.

Source: Traxsource

06

Inkswel — Play With Fire (feat. Tyla Moonchild)

Deep house · disco · boogie · Inner Tribe Records · 2026

+

The record

Play With Fire is part of Inkswel’s 2026 Resonance (Touch The Sky) project. The project was presented as a return to his house and club roots, blending deep-house textures, disco-infused grooves and cosmic dance-floor energy, with Tyla Moonchild providing the vocal.

Why it is here

I love the way this record makes the crate feel current without abandoning the old language. It is a reminder that the soulful dance-floor vocabulary keeps evolving.

Source: Inkswel / Bandcamp

07

Edramsey — Smile

Soulful house · a deliberately quieter chapter in the journey

+

The record

I have not found sufficiently reliable public documentation for the exact release history or the story behind this particular Smile, so I am not going to manufacture one for the website.

Why it is here

What matters inside the mix is the feeling: this is the release point after the deeper, heavier middle section. It lets the journey breathe before the Dwele connection arrives.

Research note: release-history documentation was not strong enough to state a definitive origin without guessing.

08

Dwele — What’s Not To Love (Conbel Evrence Soulful House Remix)

Neo-soul → soulful house · original 2010

+

The record

Dwele released What’s Not To Love in 2010 as part of W.ants W.orld W.omen. Contemporary coverage described it as a warm, romantic song about appreciating a woman for more than appearance, while the album credits list Mike City as the producer/writer of the track. The version in this session is Conbel Evrence’s soulful-house reinterpretation.

Why it is here

This is one of the clearest statements of what Vol.12 is about: take a song with a strong soul identity, then let a house producer reveal another side of it without losing the heart of the original.

Sources: Apple Music · SoulBounce · Conbel Evrence

09

Artwork Sounds — Colour of You

Contemporary South African house · CocoSA · Kabomo · Nothende · Spin Worx · 2025

+

The record

The 2025 Colour of You remix brings together Artwork Sounds, CocoSA, Kabomo, Nothende and Spin Worx. The track sits comfortably in the modern South African Afro/soul-house conversation.

Why it is here

I wanted the South African thread to return strongly after the international deep-house detour. This is not nostalgia — it is the sound continuing in real time.

Sources: Beatport · Artwork Sounds / SoundCloud

10

Rodney Hunter — Take a Ride

Electronic / disco-funk edge · G-Stone Recordings · 2005

+

The record

Take a Ride appears on Rodney Hunter’s Take a Ride Mixes, released in 2005 through G-Stone Recordings. The release collects several versions of the track and sits in the electronic side of this crate.

Why it is here

This is the left turn. After so much vocal soul, I wanted a little more movement, texture and machine-funk — something that reminds you that house has always borrowed from outside its own borders.

Sources: AllMusic · G-Stone discography archive

11

3 Winans Brothers — Dance (Louie Vega Remix)

Gospel · soulful house · The Clark Sisters · Louie Vega · 2015 remix

+

The record

The original Dance appeared on the 3 Winans Brothers’ 2014 album Foreign Land, featuring the Clark Sisters. Louie Vega then reworked it for the dance floor, with the Vega mixes released in 2015. The remix sessions included keyboards and synth bass from a team that included Josh Milan and Axel Tosca.

Why it is here

This is the spiritual centre of the mix. Gospel is not an outside influence on soulful house — it is part of its DNA. Bringing the Clark Sisters into a Louie Vega production makes that connection impossible to miss.

Sources: Traxsource · Washington Post

12

Convertion — Let’s Do It

Boogie · disco · Paradise Garage lineage · SAM Records · 1980

+

The record

Let’s Do It was released by Convertion on SAM Records in 1980, produced by Greg Carmichael with Leroy Burgess on lead vocals. It became a Paradise Garage favourite associated with Larry Levan and sits at the intersection of disco, boogie, soul and the evolving New York dance-floor language that fed house.

Why it is here

This is the historical bridge. By placing this record late in the journey, the mix can quietly reveal one of its central ideas: house did not appear from nowhere. The groove was already there.

Sources: Demon Music Group / SAM Records · Traxsource

13

Blaze — Lovely Ones

Blaze / James Toney Jr. Project · Natural Blaze · 2001

+

The record

Lovely Ones comes from Blaze’s Natural Blaze, released in 2001. The track was written by Kevin Hedge and Josh Milan and produced and mixed by Blaze; the Shelter Deep Vocal and Shelter Dub versions were part of the project’s extended life.

Why it is here

I wanted the final word to belong to Blaze. After travelling through South African house, soul, gospel, disco, neo-soul and contemporary dance music, we come back to the warmth that inspired the whole idea in the first place. Not a full stop — a door left open.

Sources: Blaze / Bandcamp · Spotify

Why these records

Different records. One conversation.

The rabbit hole is the point.

Listen to the mix once for the groove. Then come back and follow the connections: South African house to New York soulful house; gospel to house; neo-soul to dance-floor reinterpretation; disco and boogie to the language of modern deep house.

For the heads. For the dancers. For the lovers of good music.

About the research

Where release history, credits or documented context could be verified, the relevant source is linked inside the track entry. Where reliable documentation was not strong enough — notably “Smile” by Edramsey — I have deliberately left the history open rather than inventing a story. The “Why it is here” sections are Mpho’s curator perspective, not claims about what the artists intended.
